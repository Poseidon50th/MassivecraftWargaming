      Field guide

      How the battle resolves

      Read the original Version 1.3 war-system rules ↗

      1. Deployment: players alternate placing units in their designated back rows.
      2. Orders: choose three distinct units, or every remaining orderable unit if fewer than three survive.
      3. Movement: paths are orthogonal and may turn. Enemy-occupied squares block movement.
      4. Collision: higher Initiative takes a shared destination; ties send both units back.
      5. Control: compare the sum projected by both armies on every square.
      6. Casualties: a unit ending on enemy-controlled ground is destroyed.
      7. Victory: shape the map until the opposing army cannot make the required safe moves.

      Clarifications added for this playable version

      • Standard battles use three distinct orders, reduced only when fewer than three surviving units can receive orders.
      • Only mobile units count toward the required safe-move test. Artillery may rotate, but cannot supply a safe move.
      • Holding position or rotating does not count as a safe move.
      • Movement is orthogonal and may turn between steps. After choosing a destination, the player explicitly chooses a final facing before the order is confirmed. Spears, Axes, and Muskets may face in all eight directions; other units retain the four cardinal directions.
      • Self-control is always exactly the unit’s printed control strength. Hill and Fog modify only control projected away from the occupied square.
      • Movement validity uses the board at the beginning of Orders. An enemy starting square remains blocked even if its unit was ordered away.
      • Allies may pass through each other, but two allied units may not be deliberately sent to the same destination; only one unit may occupy a square.
      • A unit bounced by an enemy collision reclaims its starting square; incoming allies then bounce in a stable cascade.
      • Rotation consumes an order. Spears, Axes, and Muskets may rotate by 45-degree increments; other units rotate to a cardinal direction.
      • The browser uses fixed 8 × 8 and 16 × 16 scenarios. Computer opponents receive no access to the player's secret orders; online rooms keep both players’ committed orders on the authoritative server until simultaneous resolution.

      Special terrain

      Walls: units cannot enter; control cannot be projected onto or through them.

      Hills: +1 to control projected away from a unit standing there.

      Mud: entering or leaving spends all Movement.

      Rivers: entering or leaving applies −1 Initiative to that movement.

      Roads: a unit beginning there gains +1 Movement. Road/River intersections become bridges.

      Fog: −1 to control projected away from a unit standing there.

      Computer opponents

      Every opponent receives a sanitized copy of the public battlefield. It can see positions, facing, terrain, surviving forces, its own activation history, and the round—but never the orders you are preparing. Each commander selects a doctrine-specific army, builds mutually supporting formations, screens ranged units, reacts to threatened sectors, and tests complete simultaneous order sets. The 16 × 16 battlefield uses a time-bounded beam search; Aristides’ Codex receives the deepest deterministic search.

      Corporal · Easy: chooses a fast raiding force and uses a loose formation for reckless supported advances, dangerous terrain, and costly exchanges.

      Captain · Medium: chooses a balanced force, screens ranged units, advances in a simple battle line, and favors direct mutually destructive attacks.

      General · Hard: chooses layered combined arms, protects its formation, reacts to threatened sectors, avoids unsupported bait, and counterattacks deliberately.

      Aristides’ Codex: adapts its roster to the field and opposing force, removes intentional randomness, searches the strongest predicted replies, and presses aggressive attacks only when the formation can protect them.

      Unit guide

      Know the army

      Every diagram faces north. The center tile includes the unit’s own control, which is always equal to its printed strength. Blue tiles show movement destinations; numbered silver tiles show projected control per square. The guided tutorial includes a one-unit practice decision for every piece.
