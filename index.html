Author's Note: This is a gamified version of the war system since the concept was introduced for the War in Osc’ird that is as faithful to the document as possible all hosted using Github Pages! As someone who is still new to Massivecraft and has few connections to the roleplay world, (though I wish this to change even though I'm terrible at making introductions and usually prefer to be approached...) I've had a lot of free time to enjoy the lore and the systems they are tied with. As a result, I built this, out of interest for the war system and wishing to play around with it and poke holes in any flaws to the system to give to the staff. I now present it to you, to play with against a computer of my design. I will be expanding on this in the future, including a multiplayer and hotseat version for VCs and one person to stream it but for now, please enjoy it...I hope. Feel free to DM me if you want to RP or have any ideas on sites or games like this or feedback, I might try my hand at building them and instituting changes. For now, Aristides out!

      Field guide

      How the battle resolves

      Read the original Version 1.3 war-system rules ↗

      1. Deployment: players alternate placing units in their designated back rows.
      2. Orders: choose three distinct units, or every remaining orderable unit if fewer than three survive.
      3. Movement: paths are orthogonal and may turn. Enemy-occupied squares block movement.
      4. Collision: higher Initiative takes a shared destination; ties send both units back.
      5. Control: compare the sum projected by both armies on every square.
      6. Casualties: a unit ending on enemy-controlled ground is destroyed.
      7. Victory: shape the map until the opposing army cannot make the required safe moves.

      Clarifications added for this playable version

      • Standard battles use three distinct orders, reduced only when fewer than three surviving units can receive orders.
      • Only mobile units count toward the required safe-move test. Artillery may rotate, but cannot supply a safe move.
      • Holding position or rotating does not count as a safe move.
      • Movement is orthogonal and may turn between steps. After choosing a destination, the player explicitly chooses any north, east, south, or west final facing before the order is confirmed.
      • Self-control is always exactly the unit’s printed control strength. Hill and Fog modify only control projected away from the occupied square.
      • Movement legality uses the board at the beginning of Orders. An enemy starting square remains blocked even if its unit was ordered away.
      • Allies may pass through each other, but deliberate duplicate destinations are illegal and only one unit may occupy a square.
      • A unit bounced by an enemy collision reclaims its starting square; incoming allies then bounce in a stable cascade.
      • Rotation consumes an order. A direction is chosen explicitly: north, east, south, or west.
      • The browser uses fixed 8 × 8 and 16 × 16 scenarios and a computer opponent that receives no access to the player's secret orders.

      Unit guide

      Know the army

      Every diagram faces north. The center tile includes the unit’s own control, which is always equal to its printed strength. Blue tiles show movement destinations; numbered silver tiles show projected control per square.
